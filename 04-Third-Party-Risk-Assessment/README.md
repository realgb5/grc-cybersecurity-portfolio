# Third-Party Risk Assessment

## Overview

A simulated third-party cybersecurity risk assessment for a fictional financial services organization evaluating a critical cloud service provider.

The project demonstrates how a GRC / IT Risk professional can assess vendor security controls, identify third-party risks, evaluate evidence, prioritize findings, and develop remediation actions.

> **Disclaimer:** This is a simulated portfolio project. The organization, vendor, assessment data, evidence, findings, risks, and recommendations are fictional and created for demonstration purposes.

---

## Project Objectives

- Assess cybersecurity risks associated with a third-party vendor
- Evaluate vendor security controls and available evidence
- Identify control gaps and security weaknesses
- Assess inherent third-party risk
- Prioritize vendor-related risks
- Develop remediation recommendations
- Establish remediation owners and timelines
- Present findings in an executive-friendly format

---

## Assessment Scope

The assessment evaluates the following areas:

- Security governance
- Access control
- Data protection
- Incident response
- Business continuity and disaster recovery
- Subprocessor management
- Vulnerability management
- Security and compliance assurance

---

## Methodology

The assessment follows a structured third-party risk process:

1. Define vendor service and business criticality
2. Identify data sensitivity and assessment scope
3. Review vendor security responses and evidence
4. Evaluate control implementation
5. Identify control gaps
6. Assess inherent risk
7. Prioritize risks
8. Develop remediation actions
9. Establish owners and target timeframes
10. Determine whether residual risk requires escalation or acceptance

---

## Risk Assessment

A 5x5 likelihood and impact methodology was used to prioritize risks.

Risk score:

**Likelihood × Impact = Risk Score**

| Rating | Score |
|---|---:|
| Low | 1–4 |
| Medium | 5–9 |
| High | 10–16 |
| Critical | 17–25 |

> This scoring methodology is a portfolio methodology created for demonstration purposes and is not presented as a prescribed requirement of NIST or ISO/IEC 27001.

---

## Key Findings

The assessment identified several areas requiring remediation, including:

- Privileged-access MFA assurance
- Encryption validation
- Business continuity and disaster recovery evidence
- Subprocessor oversight
- Vendor incident notification requirements
- Vulnerability management evidence
- Independent security assurance
- Security governance documentation

---

## Remediation Approach

Remediation actions are prioritized based on risk severity and business impact.

### 0–30 Days

- Require MFA for privileged vendor access
- Validate encryption controls
- Obtain supporting security evidence

### 31–60 Days

- Review business continuity and disaster recovery testing
- Strengthen subprocessor oversight
- Validate vulnerability-management practices

### 61–90 Days

- Formalize incident notification requirements
- Establish escalation procedures
- Complete outstanding assurance documentation
